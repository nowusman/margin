## Technology Strategy and Architecture

Strategic alignment of enterprise IT investments with business capability demands.

## Technology Assets

An owned, identifiable technology item with economic value that is financially and technically controlled through its lifecycle.

A technology asset:

- Has a useful life of more than 1 year.
- Can be tangible or intangible.
- Can be active or passive.
- Supports production, services, rental, or administrative purposes.
- Provides future economic benefits.
- Has a cost that can be measured reliably.

## Technology Assets Owner

The accountable owner who plans, acquires, governs, and makes lifecycle decisions for an asset, with one owner per asset.

## Technology Asset Custodian

A role responsible for asset activities, operation, maintenance, and handover during a specific lifecycle stage.

## Assets Lifecycle Management (ALM)

Managing assets across planning, purchase, use, maintenance, and disposal to maximize useful life, reduce cost, and improve productivity.

## License Pool

A reusable collection of licenses managed by type, where released licenses return to the pool and are reassigned based on demand.

## License Entitlement

Proof that a software license has been procured by an enterprise or business unit.

## End-user license agreement (EULA)

A legal contract between a software provider and user defining the rights and restrictions governing use of the software.

## Technology Assets Tracking

A mechanism for digitally or physically tracking assets and maintaining visibility of their location and lifecycle status.

## Technology Asset Tagging

The process of attaching unique labels or tags to assets so they can be identified and tracked throughout their useful life.

## Asset Useful Life

An estimate of how many years an asset is expected to remain in service for cost-effective revenue generation.
